Valgus knee check

Pistol Rule No.2: never allow a valgus knee. Film the athlete from the front, mark their Q angle while they stand tall, and the tracker follows the standing leg through the rep: is the knee kept out over the foot, within the athlete's own limit, so they are safe to progress?

1. The athlete

The video stays on your device: only the measurements and one small picture of the knee are saved in your portfolio. Only film athletes who agreed to be filmed, for your own learning.

2. The Q angle and the rep

Film straight from the front, at knee height, with the whole body in the frame and the camera still. Open the video, pause while the athlete stands tall before the rep, with the standing knee straight (on both feet, or already on one leg), and press Use this frame for the Q angle. Click the three points on the leg they will stand on: the front of the hip bone (ASIS), the centre of the knee cap, then the bump below the knee cap (tibial tuberosity). As soon as the third point goes in, the tracker follows that leg through the rep and plays it back on a loop with the knee marked. If you move a point, the check runs again.

    3. Safe to progress?

    Mark the Q angle and check the knee to see the result.

    How far the knee goes inside the line from the hip to the ankle, as a percentage of the leg's length, through the rep. The dashed line is the limit: from this athlete's Q angle on the way down and up, and 15% at the bottom, where the thigh folds onto the shin. The shaded part is where the athlete is down on the standing leg, which is what is judged. With the video open, click the chart to go to that moment.
