Push-up coaches eye

Film an athlete from the side doing push-ups, mark the links of the body from the toes up to the hands on the floor at the top of the push-up, and the tracker follows every rep. The standard is the virtuous push-up with mastery: the hollow held from the top to the bottom, the forearms pointing straight up with the elbows in line with the hands, the head neutral. Keep reps to compare them side by side.

1. The athlete

Reps are kept in your portfolio once tracked (a short clip of just the rep), so you can play them again with the points and compare them. Only film and track athletes who agreed to be filmed, for your own learning. Kept reps are private to you; BirdBox staff only look at them if they need to, for example to help with your portfolio. Delete an observation to remove its rep.

2. Mark the links

Film from the side at floor height, with the whole body in the frame and the camera still. Open the video, pause on the top of the push-up (elbows locked out, in the hollow) and press Use this frame as the start. Click the points on the video, in order, from the toes up: the toes, ankle, knee, hip, rib cage, thoracic spine, shoulder, head, elbow, and the hand on the floor (the wrist). As soon as the points are in, the tracker follows the athlete for up to 12 seconds and finds every rep. If a point drifts, pause and drag it.

Movement

    Tracking the movement. When the last point goes in, the tracker follows the athlete from the start (for up to 12 seconds) and plays it back with the points moving on the athlete. To track a different stretch, play the video to where it should end, pause, press Set the end here, then Track again.
    Keep this rep. The rep is kept by itself after tracking. If that did not work, press Keep this rep to try again (it plays through once while it saves).

    3. What the links show

    The chart shows each link through the movement, and Where it breaks shows where a shape moves into a poor position, and what to cue or fix.

    The head strip: where the face points, from the ear to the nose: above the "level" line is looking up, below it is the chin coming down. A good strict pull-up keeps the head neutral, looking forward, a little below level the whole way; a rise well above level is the head lifting to the sky; a drop far below is the chin coming down to the chest. If an upright of the rig hides the head, check it on the video.

    The angles through the movement, from the tracked points. The white lines are the positions below. With the video open, click the chart to go to that moment.

    4. The faults I see

    Work up the chain from the hands to the feet. Tick what you see; the possible reasons and interventions open underneath.
