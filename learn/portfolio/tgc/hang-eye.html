Hang and toes to bar coaches eye

Film an athlete from the side on the bar and mark the links of the body from the toes up to the knuckles. In the hang in hollow, see the static fault and what to fix; in strict toes to bar, see where the rep moves into a poor position and what to cue or fix. Keep reps to compare them side by side.

1. The athlete

Strict toes to bar reps are kept in your portfolio once tracked (a short clip of just the rep), so you can play them again with the points and compare them. Only film and track athletes who agreed to be filmed, for your own learning. Kept reps are private to you; BirdBox staff only look at them if they need to, for example to help with your portfolio. Delete an observation to remove its rep.

2. Mark the links

Film from the side, with the whole body and the bar in the frame and the camera still. Open the video, pause on the start position (hanging in hollow) and press Use this frame as the start. Click the points on the video, in order, from the toes up. For strict toes to bar, as soon as the points are in, the tracker follows the athlete through the rep, finds the halfway and top positions, and plays the rep back with the points moving on the athlete (if the head is hidden, press Head hidden? Skip it). If a point drifts, pause and drag it. The hang in hollow is one still frame. Your video stays on your device; only the tracked points and a picture of each position are kept, privately, in your portfolio.

Movement

    Tracking the movement. When the last point goes in, the tracker follows the athlete from the start, finds the end of the rep (back in the hang) and plays it back with the points moving on the athlete. To track a different stretch, play the video to where it should end, pause, press Set the end here, then Track again.
    Keep this rep. The rep is kept by itself after tracking. If that did not work, press Keep this rep to try again (it plays through once while it saves).

    3. What the links show

    In the hang in hollow, the static shape: which link is out of position and what to fix. In strict toes to bar, the movement: where each link moves into a poor position, and what to cue or fix.

    What a good strict toes to bar looks like here (left): the hip line moves a lot, a deep dip as the hips compress to bring the toes to the bar, then back up; the shoulder line moves a little, as the lats compress, so at the top there is a big gap between the hip and shoulder lines. If the two lines come close together, the shoulder has closed and the hips have not compressed: the athlete leans back instead. The elbow, knee and toe lines stay flat near the top, as the arms, legs and toes stay straight. A dip in any of those three is where to look on the video.

    The head strip: the head should stay still in space while the body turns under it, like a chicken's head as you turn the chicken, so it is measured from where it started, not against the joints. A flat line near "still" is good; a rise is the head moving (looking up, or pushing through). If an upright of the rig hides the head (or you skipped the head point), the strip is less reliable or empty: check the head on the video.
