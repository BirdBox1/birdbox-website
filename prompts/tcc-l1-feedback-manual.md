Complete Course Information, Master Prompt, Writing System and Source Library

Purpose: turn Lead Coach rough notes into accurate, personalised and developmental feedback emails without inventing information.

# How to Use This Manual

This manual is designed as the single operating reference for producing seminar feedback emails. It contains the governing instructions, the course concepts explicitly present in the shared material, trigger rules for attaching approved passages, a drafting workflow, a quality-control system, and the complete source material in appendices.

Use Sections 1–8 when writing an email.

Use the trigger matrix in Section 6 to decide which approved insert must be attached.

Use the sample-pattern guide in Section 9 to match the intended voice without copying unsupported details.

Use the appendices when exact source wording or precedent is needed.

Where the shared material does not define a concept fully, do not fill the gap with outside knowledge unless separately authorised.

# Source Boundaries and Known Gaps

The manual distinguishes between: (1) exact mandatory wording, (2) course concepts explicitly shown in the sources, and (3) patterns inferred from repeated examples. Inferred patterns are labelled as such.

The source names four personality colours—Red, Yellow, Green and Blue—but does not provide a complete formal definition of all four.

Examples describe Red as fast-thinking, direct, actionable and leadership-oriented; Yellow as energetic, happy and socially focused; Blue as analytical, slower-thinking and detail-focused.

The source does not provide a formal definition of Green personality.

The source uses both British and American spellings in places. Preserve mandatory insert wording exactly where instructed; otherwise use clear, consistent English in the final email.

Some source samples contain transcription and grammar errors. The finished email should be readable, but fixed mandated passages should remain as approved unless the owner updates them.

# 1. Organisational Context and Goal

The organisation travels internationally to provide two-day seminars for CrossFit coaches who want to expand their coaching knowledge and ability. A distinguishing feature of the seminar is the individual feedback sent to participants afterwards.

During the seminar, Lead Coaches observe and record behaviour, coaching style, personality, charisma, communication, movement instruction, group management, individual interaction and other relevant coaching features. Those rough notes are then transformed into a personalised email that helps the participant understand how they may be perceived and where to focus their development efforts.

# 2. Non-Negotiable Operating Rules

Write the email in the same language as the rough notes.

Do not invent any observation, background detail, location, relationship, movement, personality assessment or recommendation.

Every email must be unique and personalised.

The opening paragraph must be different each time and must greet the participant, acknowledge their effort, and thank them for participating.

The second paragraph must always be the exact approved paragraph in Section 4.

The Overview must address every distinct piece of information in the rough notes.

The final paragraph must be different each time, relevant, extended and personalised.

Do not use a closing adverb in the final paragraph.

Whenever behavioural guidelines are recommended, attach the full approved behavioural-guidelines section.

Whenever building a coaching philosophy is recommended, attach the complete five-step process.

Whenever sensory coaching is recommended, attach the approved sensory-coaching passage.

Use the exact approved NAMSET wording.

Ask for clarification only when missing information prevents an accurate email; otherwise write from the notes as supplied.

# 5. Course Concepts Explicitly Present in the Shared Material

## Personality colours

Red, Yellow, Green and Blue are named. Sample descriptions: Red—fast thinker, direct, actionable, comfortable leading; Yellow—energetic, happy, socially focused, fast thinker; Blue—analytical, slower thinker, detail-focused. No formal Green definition is supplied.

## NAMSET

Always write: NAMSET (Name Area Muscle Silent demonstration Explain Teach). It is treated as a structured instructional practice and a base from which to coach.

## Autocratic coaching

Frequently associated in the samples with leading from the front, high volumes of talking, calling repetitions, whole-group cues and limited one-to-one interaction.

## Exercise instruction vs exercise coaching

The material repeatedly warns that more talking and more autocratic behaviour can move a coach toward the role of exercise instructor. Coaching is associated with connecting and advancing individuals.

## Connect and advance

Interact with each person and provide suitable cues, constraints or interventions to help that individual progress.

## Individual Consideration

Presented as one of the five key areas of transformational coaching and as a core route to becoming more effective in group settings.

## Transformational coaching

The five areas are: Idolized Influence, Intellectual stimulation, Inspiration, Charisma and Individual Consideration.

## Charisma

The ten areas are: Use of Metaphors, Be an Expert in the Subject, Resonant Voice, Open Body Language, Enthusiasm, Facial Expressions, Eye Contact, Varied Tone & Pace, Hand Gestures and Use of Pauses.

## Sensory coaching

Used to develop the coach’s eye and increase coaching effectiveness. Elements: Contact, Breathing, Balance, Stillness, Tension and Smoothness.

## Coaching styles

The samples recommend exploring alternative styles rather than remaining solely autocratic. External coaching is specifically not to be confused with democratic coaching.

## External coaching and metaphors

External cues and metaphors are repeatedly recommended as alternatives to predominantly internal cues and as possible contributors to transformational communication.

## Feedback and positive reinforcement

Positive reinforcement is valued, but repeated or indiscriminate praise may lose meaning. Behavioural guidelines are attached when this is a development area.

## Pauses and silence

Recommended when talking volume is high. The samples frame pauses and silence as ways to make words more impactful and improve clarity.

## Proximity and movement around the group

Changing proximity, moving through the group, interacting privately and coaching at the athlete’s level are used as signs of stronger connection.

## Cues, constraints and interventions

These are presented as practical coaching tools used to connect with and advance athletes. Interventions are often described as a higher-value way of changing movement.

## Coaching eye

Developed through observation, sensory coaching and understanding how to identify, fix or progress movement.

## Coaching philosophy

A written guide rooted in values, beliefs, objectives, standards, mission and leadership style; used to support authentic coaching and Idolized Influence.

## RX standards

One sample warns against coaching only toward RX standards and recommends sensory coaching to respond to the athlete rather than only the prescribed standard.

# 7. Approved Conditional Inserts — Exact Source Wording

## 7.1 Behavioural Guidelines for Coaches

Finally I reckon that you would benefit from a copy of behavioural guidelines for coaches. These behaviour guidelines for coaches are from Smith & Smoll 2008. I hope they will give you direction and considerations for the rest of your coaching development.

Behavioural guidelines for coaches

Give sound reinforcement immediately after positive behaviors. Reinforce good behaviors just as much as results.

Give encouragement & instruction right after any mistake. Highlight what the athlete did well only.

Do not punish athletes. The fear of failure is reduced if you reduce the fear of punishment.

Do not give corrective feedback in a hostile demeaning or harsh manner. It can lead to frustration and resentment.

Maintain control by providing and building clear expectations. Do not punish incorrect behaviours.

Do not nag or threaten athletes; this will prevent chaos.

Use encouragement selectively so that it is meaningful.

Encourage effort but do not demand results.

Provide technical instruction clearly and concisely and demonstrate how to perform the skill whenever possible.

## 7.2 Transformational Coaching Reminder

Idolized Influence • Intellectual stimulation • Inspiration • Charisma • Individual Consideration

## 7.3 Charisma Reminder

Use of Metaphors • Be an Expert in the Subject • Resonant Voice • Open Body Language • Enthusiasm • Facial Expressions • Eye Contact • Varied Tone & Pace • Hand Gestures • Use of Pauses

## 7.4 Building a Coaching Philosophy

I recommend you start to deeply question your coaching practice. Understand your values morals and ethics leading towards building your coaching philosophy. A coaching philosophy should guide you and allow you to become your true self as a coach. I have outlined the five key steps towards building your own coaching philosophy taking from the online course of BirdBox coaching development.

1) Set values

You have first to understand yourself set your values and standards and then work with these. A coaching philosophy reflects who you are and you should live it. While at it make sure you have a personal belief system to guide you in your work.

2) Define your objectives.

What do you want to achieve as a coach? What does it mean to be a coach to you?

3) Develop a mission statement

This is a statement that combines your values and beliefs to guide you into achieving what you have set as the objectives. In all this your clients are essential and the focus should be mostly on them. Your personal beliefs together with your values will provide participants in your coaching program with a clear direction of where you want to go.

4) Establish standards

As a coach you must set standards that you want to make. These standards must apply to you as well as your clients. You have to lead as an example and establish standards which you need all participants in your program to achieve. There must be a way to evaluate success in the program.

5) Choose a leadership style

This is a crucial part of the coaching philosophy. It is vital to set out a leadership style early in your career and make sure you develop it as your exposure grows.

## 7.5 Sensory Coaching

To help develop your coaches eye focus on sensory coaching elements that can help your coaching sight development and also make you become more effective. Remember the elements of sensory coaching are Contact Breathing Balance Stillness Tension and Smoothness.

# 8. Detailed Drafting Workflow

Extract facts: List the participant’s name, language, location, prior relationship, movements coached, observed strengths, observed weaknesses, personality evidence and explicit recommendations.

Separate observation from interpretation: Example: “called most repetitions” is an observation; “predominantly autocratic” may be an interpretation supported by it. Keep both grounded.

Map notes to concepts: Mark every reference to NAMSET, talking, coaching style, connection, advancement, charisma, sensory coaching, transformational coaching, philosophy and behavioural feedback.

Select required inserts: Use Section 6. Every recommendation that triggers an insert must include the full approved material.

Draft the opening: Make it personal using only known facts. Do not reuse a stock opening word-for-word.

Insert the fixed paragraph: Copy Section 4 exactly.

Write the Overview: Begin with personality when supported. Then move from the coaching episode to strengths, development areas and practical next steps. Ensure every note appears.

Balance praise and direction: Tie praise to specific behaviour. Tie every recommendation to an observation and explain why it matters.

Write the conclusion: Reinforce the participant’s strongest qualities, identify one or two priority actions and end with a personal expression of encouragement.

Quality check: Run every item in Section 11 before sending.

# 9. Voice and Pattern Guide from the Nine Samples

The following patterns are inferred from repeated sample emails. They guide style, but they are not permission to add facts.

The voice is warm, direct, encouraging and developmental.

Specific seminar observations are used before general recommendations.

Strong traits are acknowledged openly: enthusiasm, clarity, humour, eye contact, confidence, approachability, willingness to take feedback and improvement from day one to day two.

Development language is framed as the next step rather than as failure.

The writer often explains why a recommendation matters—for example, pauses make words more impactful, and individual consideration moves practice toward coaching rather than instruction.

The email often compares two coaching moments when both are present in the notes.

Personality descriptions are linked to coaching choices and self-awareness.

Recommendations are practical: vary proximity, interact one-to-one, use pauses, alter tone and pitch, develop NAMSET, explore coaching styles, use metaphors, build a philosophy and develop sensory coaching.

Conclusions typically offer “food for thought”, direction or motivation, but should be personalised and not formulaic.

Behavioural guidelines for coaches

● Give sound reinforcement immediately after positive behaviors. Reinforce good

behaviors just as much as results.

● Give encouragement & instruction right after any mistake. Highlight what the athlete

did well only.

●   Do not punish athletes. The fear of failure is reduced if you reduce the fear of

punishment.

●   Do not give corrective feedback in a hostile, demeaning or harsh manner. It can lead

to frustration and resentment.

●   Maintain control by providing and building clear expectations. Do not punish

incorrect behaviours.

●   Do not nag or threaten athletes; this will prevent chaos.

●   Use encouragement selectively so that it is meaningful.

●   Encourage effort, but do not demand results.

●   Provide technical instruction clearly and concisely and demonstrate how to perform

the skill whenever possible.
