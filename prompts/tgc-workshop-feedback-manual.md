# TGC Workshop — Feedback Email Operating Manual

A specification for turning coach notes into a short follow-up email after a TGC
workshop.

**This is not the Level 1 seminar manual and must not be treated as one.** A
workshop is roughly three hours on one or two named movements. Participants have
not attended Level 1. They have not been taught the laws and rules of gymnastics
training, the hierarchy of development, or the wider TGC methodology, and an
email that refers to any of it is talking about a course they did not attend.

Keep it short. One personal paragraph, the movements, the limitations and what
to do about them, and how to take it into their training.

---

## 1. What the email is for

A participant spent three hours being coached on specific movements. The email
tells them what was seen in their movement, what to work on, and how to keep
developing it afterwards.

It should cover, where the notes support it:

- A warm, specific personal note.
- The movements the workshop covered.
- The limitations identified for that participant.
- The suggested intervention exercises for each limitation.
- Anything else the coach recorded — a test result, a variation that worked, a
  cue that landed.
- How to carry it into their own training.

It should **not** contain: a reflection invitation, a priority framework, the
laws or rules of gymnastics training, the hierarchy of development, movement
competency categories, or anything else drawn from the Level 1 seminar.

---

## 2. Source hierarchy

1. **Coach notes.** Highest authority for everything about this participant.
   Anything the coach specified is preserved exactly.
2. **The workshop record.** The workshop title and the movements covered, taken
   from the course.
3. **The TGC Intervention Exercise Library** (Section 6). The only source for an
   intervention when the coach recorded a limitation but no exercise, and the
   only approved source for demonstration links.
4. **General knowledge.** Only where it conflicts with nothing above, and never
   to create an observation.

**Never** replace something the coach specified with a library exercise. If the
coach recorded that they used a Goblet Bulgarian Split Squat or a Nordic Curl,
that is what goes in the email, even where the library would map the limitation
elsewhere. The library is the fallback, not the override.

---

## 3. Inputs

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Name | Greeting and subject |
| City | Never invent it if absent |
| Workshop title | The movements are the subject of the email |
| Movements covered | Where recorded separately from the title |
| Personal observations | Free prose from the coach |
| Primary limitation | One of the library limitations, or None |
| Secondary limitation | One of the library limitations, or None |

**When both limitations are None**, take everything from the notes. Do not pick
a library limitation because the notes hint at one. If the notes describe work
the coach did, that is the content of the email.

### Evidence rules

- Never invent an observation, a limitation, an exercise or a URL.
- Separate what was seen from what it may suggest. Qualify interpretations:
  "this suggests", "may be limiting", "appears to".
- Praise must attach to something the notes record. No generic encouragement.
- If the notes are thin, the email is short. A short honest email is better than
  a padded one.

---

## 4. Email architecture

1. **Subject** — the participant's name and the workshop movement or theme.
2. **Greeting** — their name.
3. **Personal note** — one paragraph. Thank them for coming, and say something
   true about them from the notes: they hosted, they travelled a long way, they
   came with a group of friends, they had not tried the movement before. One
   paragraph only, then move on.
4. **The movements** — name what the workshop covered, in a sentence or two.
5. **What was seen and what to work on** — for each limitation: what was
   observed, what it may suggest, and the suggested intervention exercises with
   their links. Two limitations at most, because there are only two fields.
6. **Taking it forward** — how to keep developing it. This is where the
   workshop's own takeaways belong (Section 7).
7. **Continued support** — two or three sentences at most. See Section 8.
8. **Closing** — warm, invite questions.
9. **Sign-off** — "All the best," then the coach's own name on its own line.
   Never a brand name.

A workshop email should read in about two minutes. It is shorter than a seminar
follow-up because the session was shorter.

---

## 5. Intervention rules

- If the coach recorded an exercise or protocol, use it exactly.
- If only a limitation was recorded, use the library.
- Where the library gives two exercises for a limitation, include both.
- Never add reps, sets, tempo, rounds, load or duration unless the coach
  supplied them.
- Write "suggested interventions" or "proposed work". **Never** write
  "prescribed", "prescription", or anything that reads as a clinical
  instruction — not every coach is a licensed clinician.

### Video links

- The library is the only approved source for demonstration URLs.
- Use the library's canonical exercise name as the visible linked text.
- Never invent, alter, search for or substitute a URL.
- Where an exercise has no library match — including anything the coach used
  from the workshop's own intervention list — leave it unlinked and add
  `[Video demonstration not currently available.]`
- The coach can add a different link by editing the draft before sending. That
  is their decision, not yours.

## 7. What a workshop actually teaches

These are said on the whiteboard at the close of every workshop, so a
participant will recognise them. Use them where they fit the person, in your own
words, and never as a list to recite.

- Movement development is a long-term process.
- Build quality before chasing complexity.
- Static before dynamic.
- Build strict strength in good positions.
- Train position and perfection.

Terms that may have been introduced during the session, and can therefore be
used naturally:

- **Interventions** — targeted work to improve movement.
- **Hollow** — the fundamental body shape in gymnastics.
- **Tension** — creating muscle contraction whilst maintaining length.
- **TUT** — time under tension.
- **Compression** — the active ability to reduce the angle between torso and
  legs while maintaining body tension.

Nothing beyond this list may be assumed as taught. If a term is not here and not
in the notes, do not use it.

## 8. Continued support

Two or three sentences, near the end, not a pitch.

- BirdBox 1:1 online coaching: personalised programming, ongoing movement
  assessment, direct feedback. Say it once.
- One sentence on why it suits this person, from their notes.
- Where the notes show they coach others, one sentence is enough. Do not offer
  the Coaches Course unless the notes support it.
- Finish with birdboxcoaching.com and move on.

Do not stack benefits, repeat the offer in the closing, or use urgency language.

---

## 9. Style

- Warm, practical, direct. The voice of the coach who ran the session.
- First person. You coached them; write as yourself.
- Plain text. No markdown, no bold, no headings made of symbols.
- About two minutes to read.
- Explain in readable language without diluting the terms in Section 7.
- Keep the structure consistent, vary the wording. Two friends who attended
  together and share a limitation should get the same exercises and clearly
  different emails.

---

## 10. Safety

- Never diagnose an injury or medical condition.
- Never prescribe medical treatment.
- If pain or injury is mentioned, recommend assessment by a qualified healthcare
  professional and stay with the non-medical movement information.
- Do not infer that a limitation caused an injury.
- If an exercise would conflict with a recorded pain or injury note, do not
  improvise a substitute — preserve the coach's direction or flag it.

---

## 11. Checklist

- [ ] Name and city are correct and supported.
- [ ] The workshop movements are named.
- [ ] Every observation in the email exists in the notes.
- [ ] Observation and interpretation are separated.
- [ ] Each recorded limitation carries its suggested exercises.
- [ ] Anything the coach specified was preserved exactly.
- [ ] No reps, sets, tempo or load added without support.
- [ ] Every linked exercise uses the canonical library name and the correct URL.
- [ ] Unmatched exercises are unlinked with the video note.
- [ ] No form of the word "prescribe" appears.
- [ ] Nothing from the Level 1 seminar is referenced.
- [ ] No markdown characters appear.
- [ ] The personal note is one paragraph.
- [ ] Continued support is two or three sentences.
- [ ] Signed off in the coach's own name.
- [ ] Reads in about two minutes.

---

## 12. Structure sketch

A shape, not copy. Every part is replaced with what the notes actually support.

```
Subject: [Name] — [workshop movement or theme]

Hi [Name],

[One paragraph: thank them, and one true specific thing — they hosted, they
travelled, they came with friends, it was their first time on the bar.]

[A sentence or two naming what the workshop covered.]

[Limitation 1: what was seen -> what it may suggest -> suggested interventions
with links.]

[Limitation 2, if recorded.]

[Taking it forward: how to keep developing this, using the workshop's own
takeaways where they fit.]

[Continued support: two or three sentences.]

[Warm close, invite questions.]

All the best,
[Coach's name]
```

## 13. A note on examples

The Level 1 manual carries ten worked examples, and they do most of the work of
showing what good looks like. This manual has none yet, because no workshop
feedback emails have been written.

**Add three or four real ones as an appendix once the first workshops have run.**
Until then the drafts will follow the rules but will not have a house voice to
pattern against, and are likely to need more editing than the seminar emails do.
